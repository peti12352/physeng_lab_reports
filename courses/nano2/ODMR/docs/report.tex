\begin{abstract}
This report details the implementation and results of Time-Resolved Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance (TR-ODMR) measurements on Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers in a bulk diamond sample. We performed Continuous Wave (CW) ODMR to characterize zero-field splitting and magnetic field response. Time-resolved measurements were conducted to observe Rabi oscillations and determine the longitudinal relaxation time $T_1$.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy center (NV$^-$) in diamond is a point defect formed by a substitutional nitrogen atom next to a vacancy. Its electronic ground state is a spin triplet ($S=1$) with a zero-field splitting between the $m_s=0$ and $m_s=\pm 1$ sublevels of approximately $D\approx2.87$~GHz at room temperature. In the effective spin-1 description used in this work, the ground-state Hamiltonian can be written as
\begin{equation}
\frac{H}{h}=D S_z^2 + E\left(S_x^2-S_y^2\right)+\gamma_e\,\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{S},
\end{equation} 
\cite{loenard2025limitsabsolutevectormagnetometry} 
where $E$ describes strain- and electric-field-induced splitting, \(\gamma_e\) is the electron gyromagnetic ratio, and \(\mathbf{B}\) is the applied magnetic field.

ODMR combines optical spin polarization/readout with microwave-driven magnetic resonance. Under green excitation, spin-selective intersystem crossing pumps the NV ensemble preferentially into $m_s=0$, and the fluorescence is higher for $m_s=0$ than for $m_s=\pm1$. When the microwave drive is resonant with a spin transition, population is transferred out of $m_s=0$ and a dip appears in fluorescence. In zero magnetic field this produces the characteristic two-peak structure around $D$ due to nonzero $E$.

After CW frequency calibration, we used time-resolved pulse sequences to probe longitudinal relaxation and coherent control. The $T_1$ sequence measures recovery toward thermal equilibrium as a function of delay $\tau$, while the Rabi represent coherent spin rotations driven by resonant microwave pulses of variable duration. The measured signals were fitted with the models
\begin{equation}
V_{T_1}(\tau)=A\exp\left(-\tau/T_1\right)+C,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
V_R(\tau)=A\exp\left[-\left(\tau/T_2^{\mathrm{eff}}\right)^\beta\right]\sin\left(\omega_{\mathrm{Rabi}}\tau+\phi\right)+B\tau+C.
\end{equation}

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Photoluminescence and Lock-in Calibration}

We carried out our measurements in workstation 1 using the D9 sample.
During setup alignment, the detector background level before laser excitation was approximately $0.22$~V, while the best photoluminescence operating point reached about $1.5$~V. The sample position was adjusted to find the region of highest density of nitrogen vacancy sites by maximizing photoluminescence signal at a voltmeter.

For the ODMR measurements we slightly re-calibrated the sample position to optimize for the lock-in signal, which measures the difference in photoluminescence between the microwave excitation time and the non-microwave excitation time. The sample's concentration of nitrogen vacancies and the microwave's intensity are inhomogeneous in space.We find the position that with both the highest concentration of nitrogen vacancies and highest intensity of the microwave field. We could reach a maximum signal level of $18.5$~mV, with a noise floor of $0.1$~mV. We used a lock-in time constant of 10 ms.

\subsection{CW ODMR and Magnetic Field Measurements}

Initially, a frequency sweep was conducted in zero magnetic field to characterize the ground-state splitting of the NV centers (Fig.~\ref{fig:cw_zerofield}). The resonance exhibits a double-peak structure due to the strain-induced crystal field parameter $E$.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/CW_SweepREDO_analysis.pdf}
    \caption{CW ODMR sweep at zero magnetic field from 2.3 to 3.4 GHz range with extracted $D$ and $E$ parameters indicated by the fit.}
    \label{fig:cw_zerofield}
\end{figure}

By analyzing this splitting, we extracted $D = 2.87$~GHz and the splitting parameter $E = 3.25$~GHz. The obtained $D$ value is consistent with the standard literature value for NV$^-$ centers at room temperature.

In the zero-field scan, two resonance peaks are observed, corresponding to the $m_s=0\rightarrow m_s=+1$ and $m_s=0\rightarrow m_s=-1$ transitions split by $E$. $D$ is the average of the two resonance frequencies and $E$ is half of their separation.

After enabling microwave modulation and lock-in detection, the optimization target became fluorescence contrast between microwave-on and microwave-off conditions. The best lock-in amplitude was approximately $R\approx18.5$~mV with a noise floor of about $0.1$~mV. This behavior is consistent with spatial inhomogeneity in both NV density and microwave field strength across the sample and coplanar waveguide.

We apply a arbitrary direction magnetic field and re-do the sweep as can be seen in Fig.~\ref{fig:magnet_cal}(a). With an external magnetic field, the spectrum is expected to show up to eight resonances: four crystallographic NV orientations, each with two allowed transitions. In our data, the observed spectrum is consistent with this orientation-split ODMR picture, although 6 peaks only can be clearly seen. A focused sweep of one peak (Fig.~\ref{fig:magnet_cal}(b)) was then used to identify the working resonance frequency at $3.0912$~GHz, which was used for the time-resolved measurements. We used a lock-in time constant of 30 ms to improve stability.

\subsection{Rabi Oscillations}

Coherent control of the NV spin was investigated using the Rabi oscillation pulse sequence. Our group's experimental results are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:rabi_ours}. While clear sinusoidal oscillations were limited in our physical setup, the transition to saturation and the characteristic damping times were successfully captured by the fitting model. The fitting was done according to the formula: 

$V_R(\tau) = A e^{-(\tau / T_2^{\text{eff}})^\beta} \sin (\omega_{\text{Rabi}} \tau + \phi) + B\tau + C$
\begin{description}
    \item[$T_2^{\text{eff}}$] The effective relaxation time characterizing the dephasing timescale of the NV center ensemble. 
    \item[$\beta$] A dimensionless empirical parameter that accounts for the inhomogeneous sample and MW field. \footnote{There is an ensemble of NV centers, and each has a slightly different environment and Rabi frequency because of the inhomogeneity. This parameter accounts for the spread of the distribution of frequencies, and is less than 1. In an ideal case it would be 1.}
    \item[$\omega_{\text{Rabi}}$] The Rabi frequency, the angular rate at which the spin state is driven between the $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ states. It is directly proportional to the amplitude of the applied microwave magnetic field.
    \item[$B$] A coefficient introduced to model temperature-dependent baseline drift during the measurement due to the MW power. 
\end{description}

As expected, $\omega_{\mathrm{Rabi}}$ increases with the power. The few data points and the non ideality of the MW and amplifier explain the deviation from the linear fit. On the other hand $T_2^{\text{eff}}$ decreases with an increasing power. This is manily due to the effect of the inhomogeneous MW field, which creates a spread of $\omega_{\mathrm{Rabi}}$, which macroscopically destructively interfere making the dephasing quicker. This is in accordance with the measurements.

\subsection{T1 Relaxation Time}

The longitudinal relaxation time $T_1$ was measured using a variable-delay sequence. Figure~\ref{fig:t1_plots} compares our group's cleanest experimental dataset with the reference sweep provided by the laboratory instructor. The signal follows an exponential decay, $V(\tau)=A\exp(-\tau/T_1)+C$. 

The sequence design followed the standard timing constraints for this setup, with long initialization and short readout pulses (nominally on the order of $t_{\mathrm{init}}\sim50\,\mu$s and $t_{\mathrm{read}}\sim10\,\mu$s). The polarized state yields a higher fluorescence level than the relaxed state, giving the positive contrast required for robust $T_1$ fitting.

\section{Conclusion}

We successfully implemented TR-ODMR characterization of NV centers in diamond. By extracting the zero-field splitting $D$ and $E$, calibrating the magnetic field response, and identifying the optimal modulation frequency at 3.0912 GHz, we established a robust framework for spin manipulation. Our results, validated against colleague and instructor data, provide a clear quantification of the coherent nutation and relaxation regimes of the system.
