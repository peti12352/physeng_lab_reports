\begin{abstract}
This report details the implementation and results of Time-Resolved Optically Detected Magnetic Resonance (TR-ODMR) measurements on Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers in a bulk diamond sample. We performed Continuous Wave (CW) ODMR to characterize zero-field splitting and magnetic field response. Time-resolved measurements were conducted to observe Rabi oscillations and determine the longitudinal relaxation time $T_1$. Despite experimental challenges in microwave delivery, we successfully extracted the core spin Hamiltonian parameters and validated our results against reference datasets.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}

The Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) center in diamond is a point defect consisting of a substitutional nitrogen atom adjacent to a lattice vacancy. Its ground state is a spin triplet ($S=1$), where the $m_s=0$ state is separated from the $m_s=\pm 1$ states by a zero-field splitting $D \approx 2.87$~GHz. In this report, we document the calibration of the CW resonances and the subsequent investigation of coherent spin dynamics and relaxation.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{CW ODMR and Magnetic Field Calibration}

Initially, a frequency sweep was conducted in zero magnetic field to characterize the ground-state splitting of the NV centers (Fig.~\ref{fig:cw_zerofield}). The resonance exhibits a double-peak structure due to the strain-induced crystal field parameter $E$.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/CW_SweepREDO_analysis.pdf}
    \caption{CW ODMR spectrum at zero magnetic field (2.3 - 3.4 GHz range).}
    \label{fig:cw_zerofield}
\end{figure}

By analyzing this splitting, we extracted the zero-field splitting parameters $D$ and $E$. These benchmarks are overlaid on the full magnetic field sweep in Fig.~\ref{fig:magnet_cal}a. A focused sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:magnet_cal}b) was then used to accurately identify the optimal resonance frequency at $3.0912$~GHz, which was used for all subsequent pulsed manipulation tasks.

\section{Conclusion}

We successfully implemented TR-ODMR characterization of NV centers in diamond. By extracting the zero-field splitting $D$ and $E$, calibrating the magnetic field response, and identifying the optimal modulation frequency at 3.0912 GHz, we established a robust framework for spin manipulation. Our results, validated against colleague and instructor data, provide a clear quantification of the coherent nutation and relaxation regimes of the system.
