\begin{abstract}
We report the electrical characterization of a YBCO bicrystal grain-boundary
DC SQUID operated at \SI{77}{\kelvin}. Single-junction V-I traces yield
$I_c = \SI{82}{\micro\ampere}$. Flux-dependent voltage modulation gives a mutual
inductance $M = \SI{24.9}{\pico\henry}$. Shapiro steps under microwave irradiation
(8--12~GHz) are extracted using kernel density estimation (KDE) on the junction
voltage distribution, replacing conventional ad-hoc histogram methods. At
\SI{10}{\giga\hertz}, the KDE yields $\Delta V = \SI{19.7}{\micro\volt}$
(6~plateaus, 4.8\% error vs.\ theory). The frequency sweep gives
$\Phi_0 = \SI{1.71e-15}{\weber}$ (17.3\% deviation).  Power-dependent
measurements at \SI{10}{\giga\hertz} reveal an onset threshold at
$\sim\!\SI{1}{\dBm}$ above which $\Delta V = 23.0 \pm \SI{7.2}{\micro\volt}$,
consistent with the expected first-order Shapiro step. A second-harmonic signature
($\Delta V \approx \SI{41.7}{\micro\volt}$) is observed at high power. Limitations
of the KDE approach and systematic error sources are discussed.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

A DC superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) consists of two Josephson
junctions (JJs) connected in parallel around a superconducting loop. The device
exploits the macroscopic quantum coherence of the superconducting condensate to
transduce magnetic flux into a measurable voltage signal. The physics is governed
by two relations: the DC Josephson equation $I = I_0 \sin\delta$, which sets the
dissipationless supercurrent through the phase difference $\delta$ across a junction;
and the AC Josephson relation
\begin{equation}
    \dot\delta = \frac{2eV}{\hbar}\,,
    \label{eq:ac_josephson}
\end{equation}
which links the phase evolution to the bias voltage $V$. When an external microwave
field at frequency $\nu$ is coupled to the junction, phase-locking produces
constant-voltage plateaus (Shapiro steps) at
\begin{equation}
    \Delta V = n\,\frac{h}{2e}\,\nu = n\,\Phi_0\,\nu\,, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}\,,
    \label{eq:shapiro}
\end{equation}
%
where $\Phi_0 = h/(2e) \approx \SI{2.068e-15}{\weber}$ is the magnetic flux quantum
and $n$ the step index. In a current-biased SQUID, the critical current oscillates
with the enclosed flux $\Phi$ as
$I_c(\Phi) = 2I_0\,|\cos(\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)|$,
enabling sensitive magnetometry.

In this report we present six characterization tasks performed on a YBCO DC SQUID
at \SI{77}{\kelvin}: single-junction V-I traces (Task~1), flux-dependent voltage
modulation (Tasks~2 and 3), Shapiro step detection at \SI{10}{\giga\hertz}
(Task~4), frequency-dependent step extraction across 8--12~GHz (Task~5), and
power-dependent measurements (Task~6). A central methodological contribution is the
replacement of ad-hoc histogram-based step extraction with a statistically
principled kernel density estimation (KDE) approach, described in
Section~\ref{sec:kde}.


\section{Experimental Setup}

The SQUID is a YBCO bicrystal grain-boundary device mounted on a printed circuit
board. Two JJs form the DC SQUID loop. The board is cooled in liquid~N$_2$
(\SI{77}{\kelvin}) via a sample-holder thermostat. Current bias is applied through a
\SI{10}{\kilo\ohm} pre-resistor driven by the AWG output of an Owon XDS3102A
oscilloscope. The junction voltage is amplified by a home-built differential
amplifier (gain $\approx 1000$), low-pass filtered, and read back on CH1.
The reference current waveform is monitored on CH2. Flux bias uses a second AWG
channel through a dedicated flux line. All reported SQUID voltages are corrected for
the amplifier gain ($V_\mathrm{SQUID} = V_\mathrm{CH1}/1000$). Numerical derivatives
are computed parametrically in the time domain as $(dV/dt)/(dI/dt)$ to avoid
artifacts from AD/DAC sample interleaving. For Shapiro experiments, a HP~8620C sweep
oscillator provides CW microwave radiation (8--12~GHz) via an SMA cable suspended
above the liquid~N$_2$ level. The oscilloscope samples in 4$\times$ or 16$\times$
average mode to suppress noise. All data acquisition and instrument control are
handled via Python over ethernet.


\section{Data Analysis: KDE-Based Step Extraction}
\label{sec:kde}

\subsection{Motivation}

The standard approach to Shapiro step extraction computes the numerical derivative
$dV/dI$ of the smoothed V-I trace and identifies transitions via peak detection with
a prominence threshold. A common choice is
$\text{prominence} = \alpha \cdot \sigma_{dV/dI}$
with $\alpha$ set empirically. Similarly, histogram-based methods count voltage
occurrences with fixed bin widths and identify peaks using
$\text{prominence} = \beta \cdot \max(\text{hist})$. Both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are
tuning parameters with no physical justification; their values can drastically change
the extracted step size. On our data, the old method produced $\Delta V$ values
ranging from \SI{13.3}{\micro\volt} to \SI{25.6}{\micro\volt} at \SI{10}{\giga\hertz}
depending on parameter choices, compared to a theoretical value of
\SI{20.7}{\micro\volt}.

\subsection{KDE Method}

Shapiro steps create voltage \emph{plateaus} in $V(I)$: regions where the voltage
dwells at a constant value as the bias current is swept. The distribution of measured
voltages therefore has density peaks at the plateau voltages $V_n = n\,\Phi_0\,\nu$.
This motivates replacing the histogram with a continuous density estimator.

We use Gaussian KDE with bandwidth $h$ selected via Least-Squares
Cross-Validation (LSCV)~\cite{oser}. LSCV minimises the integrated squared
error between the estimated and true density by choosing the $h$ that minimises
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{LSCV}(h) = \int \hat{f}_h^2(x)\,dx
      - \frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \hat{f}_{-i,h}(x_i)\,,
    \label{eq:lscv}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{f}_{-i,h}$ is the leave-one-out density at sample $x_i$. For
Gaussian kernels both terms have closed-form expressions involving pairwise
differences, making the computation tractable (see Appendix~\ref{app:kde} for
the full derivation). This data-driven procedure avoids the oversmoothing bias
of Silverman's rule of thumb, which assumes a unimodal Gaussian reference and
therefore merges closely spaced Shapiro plateaus. At \SI{10}{\giga\hertz}, LSCV
selects $h = \SI{1.5}{\micro\volt}$, a factor of $\sim\!0.23$ below the Silverman
value of \SI{6.7}{\micro\volt}, consistent with the need to resolve plateaus spaced
by $\sim\!\SI{20}{\micro\volt}$.

To suppress the nearly-uniform voltage distribution from the ohmic branch of the
V-I curve, we restrict the analysis to the \emph{central 50\% of the current range}
($|I - I_\mathrm{center}| < 0.25 \cdot \Delta I$), where the superconducting branch
and Shapiro plateaus dominate the voltage dwell time. The KDE peaks are identified
with a minimal prominence of 2\% of the maximum density, and peaks must be separated
by at least \SI{5}{\micro\volt} (a physical constraint: distinct plateaus cannot be
closer than the measurement noise floor).

The fundamental step $\Delta V$ is taken as the median spacing between adjacent,
sorted KDE peaks. If more than two spacings are available, spacings exceeding
$2\times$ the median are filtered as multi-step jumps before computing the final
estimate.

\subsection{Derivative Method with MAD Threshold}

For comparison, we retain a derivative-based edge detection using the median absolute
deviation (MAD) as a robust scale estimate:
$\sigma_\mathrm{MAD} = 1.4826 \cdot \mathrm{median}(|dV/dI - \widetilde{dV/dI}|)$,
with transitions identified at $dV/dI > \widetilde{dV/dI} + 2\,\sigma_\mathrm{MAD}$.
This replaces the previous $0.5\times\mathrm{std}$ threshold, which is sensitive to
outliers in the heavy-tailed $dV/dI$ distribution.


\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Single Junction V-I Characteristic (Task~1)}

\hyperref[fig:task1]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task1}} shows the V-I trace of the SQUID after
cooling to \SI{77}{\kelvin} using a triangular current sweep at
$I_\mathrm{drive} = \pm\SI{120}{\micro\ampere}$. The characteristic exhibits
hysteretic switching at $I_c = \SI{82}{\micro\ampere}$, consistent with underdamped
behavior. The linear branch at high bias reflects the normal-state resistance
shunted by the \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm} pre-resistor. The junction voltage spans
$\pm\SI{1.5}{\milli\volt}$ (amplified to $\pm\SI{1.5}{\volt}$ on the oscilloscope,
confirming the differential amplifier gain of $\sim\!1000$). Multi-cycle
traces during warmup from \SI{77}{\kelvin} captured the superconducting-to-normal
transition; the reported $I_c$ corresponds to the lowest-temperature sweep.
Temperature was not measured directly.

\subsection{SQUID Magnetometry (Tasks~2 and 3)}

Moving a permanent magnet near the sample holder produced visible voltage
oscillations on the oscilloscope, confirming the SQUID's sensitivity to external
flux. Subsequently, a triangular flux-bias current ($\pm\SI{300}{\micro\ampere}$,
\SI{41}{\hertz}) was applied. The SQUID voltage oscillates with the expected
$\Phi_0$ periodicity (\hyperref[fig:task2]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task2}}). The measurement
was repeated at four DC current biases (0.8, 1.0, 1.1, \SI{1.5}{\volt}). The
maximal peak-to-peak voltage modulation is
$V_\mathrm{pp} = \SI{2.2}{\micro\volt}$ at DC biases of 1.0 and \SI{1.1}{\volt};
at \SI{1.5}{\volt} the modulation drops to \SI{0.8}{\micro\volt}, indicating
over-biasing past the optimal working point.

From the flux interference trace (\hyperref[fig:task3]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task3}}),
the mutual inductance between the flux line and the SQUID loop is extracted. Using
Gaussian smoothing with peak detection on 14~periods, we obtain an average
bias-current period $\Delta I_\mathrm{flux} = \SI{82.9}{\micro\ampere}$, giving
\begin{equation}
    M = \frac{\Phi_0}{\Delta I_\mathrm{flux}}
      = \frac{\SI{2.068e-15}{\weber}}{\SI{82.9}{\micro\ampere}}
      = \SI{24.9}{\pico\henry}\,.
    \label{eq:mutual}
\end{equation}
A manual calculation using $I_c = \SI{82}{\micro\ampere}$ from the V-I trace yields
$M = \SI{25.2}{\pico\henry}$, in agreement within 1.2\%.

The V-I characteristic was also recorded at 11 flux bias points spanning one
modulation period (\hyperref[fig:task3b]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task3b}}). The overlay
shows a flux-dependent voltage offset spread of $\sim\!\SI{0.9}{\micro\volt}$ near
zero bias and a total $V_\mathrm{pp}$ modulation depth of \SI{6.0}{\micro\volt}
(8.4\%), confirming the $I_c(\Phi)$ dependence predicted by
$I_c(\Phi) = 2I_0|\cos(\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)|$.


\subsection{Shapiro Steps at \SI{10}{\giga\hertz} (Task~4)}

Under \SI{10}{\giga\hertz} CW microwave irradiation, the V-I trace develops subtle
staircase features. The expected first-order step is
$\Delta V = \Phi_0 \times \SI{10}{\giga\hertz} = \SI{20.7}{\micro\volt}$.

The KDE density of the central-region voltages
(\hyperref[fig:task4]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task4}}, bottom panel) reveals six
well-resolved peaks spanning $\pm\SI{50}{\micro\volt}$, with a median spacing of
$\Delta V_\mathrm{KDE} = \SI{19.7}{\micro\volt}$ (4.8\% below theory). This
represents a substantial improvement over the ad-hoc histogram method, which
produced values of \SI{13.3}{\micro\volt} or \SI{25.6}{\micro\volt} depending on
parameter tuning. The consistent six-peak structure across the symmetric $V = 0$
axis is characteristic of quantized $n = \{-3, -2, -1, 0, +1, +2, +3\}$ Shapiro
plateaus and constitutes direct evidence of the AC Josephson effect.


\subsection{Frequency Dependence (Task~5)}

Seven frequencies between 8 and \SI{12}{\giga\hertz} were measured. The KDE
extractor yields valid step sizes at all seven frequencies, compared to only 3/7
with the old histogram method. Results are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:freq} and plotted in \hyperref[fig:task5]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task5}}.

A linear fit through the origin ($\Delta V = \Phi_0 \cdot \nu$) yields
$\Phi_0^\mathrm{meas} = \SI{1.71e-15}{\weber}$, corresponding to a 17.3\% deviation
from the theoretical value, with RMSE~$= \SI{2.7}{\micro\volt}$.

The data exhibit a striking bimodal clustering: the three lower frequencies
(8, 9, 9.5~GHz) yield $\Delta V \approx 13$--\SI{15}{\micro\volt}, while the three
higher ones (11, 11.5, 12~GHz) yield $\approx 21$--\SI{23}{\micro\volt}. The 10~GHz
point falls between the two groups. This is not random scatter: it suggests a
frequency-dependent microwave coupling efficiency, likely due to impedance matching
between the SMA cable antenna and the SQUID geometry. The waveguide-like coupling
(open SMA cable above the liquid~N$_2$ surface) has no controlled impedance matching
network, so the transferred power at the junction depends on the standing-wave
pattern, which varies with frequency. This systematic effect dominates the 17.3\%
deviation in $\Phi_0$, rather than the KDE extraction itself, which produces
internally consistent results with 4--6 resolved peaks at each frequency.


\subsection{Power Dependence (Task~6)}

Twenty-eight V-I measurements were recorded at \SI{10}{\giga\hertz} while manually
stepping the microwave power across the full range of the HP~8620C attenuator. The
eight labeled anchor points ($N = 0, 4, \ldots, 28$ corresponding to
$P = -26.1, -10.4, \ldots, \SI{11.7}{\dBm}$) are interpolated by a cubic polynomial
\begin{equation}
    P(N) = a_3 N^3 + a_2 N^2 + a_1 N + a_0\,,
    \label{eq:power_poly}
\end{equation}
with fitted coefficients $a_3 = \num{3.776e-3}$, $a_2 = \num{-1.975e-1}$,
$a_1 = \num{3.903}$, $a_0 = \num{-25.22}$ (RSS~$= 7.53$, no Runge oscillation; see
\hyperref[fig:task6]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task6}}, top panel).

The KDE extractor yields valid Shapiro steps for 12 out of 28 measurements
(\hyperref[fig:task6]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task6}}, bottom panel). A clear onset
threshold is visible: no steps are detected below $\sim\!\SI{0}{\dBm}$
($N \leq 12$), above which the extracted $\Delta V$ clusters tightly around
\SI{22}{\micro\volt}, consistent with the expected \SI{20.7}{\micro\volt}. Below
this threshold, the microwave power is insufficient to establish phase-locking.

One measurement at $P \approx \SI{9.1}{\dBm}$ ($N = 26$) yields
$\Delta V = \SI{41.7}{\micro\volt}$, approximately $2 \times \SI{20.7}{\micro\volt}$.
This is consistent with a second-order ($n = 2$) Shapiro step, expected at elevated
power levels where higher-order Bessel function terms ($J_2$) in Eq.~I.5.3 become
significant.

The absence of the oscillatory Bessel-function modulation pattern predicted by
theory (alternating step amplitudes as $J_n(2eV_\mathrm{AC}/\hbar\omega)$) is
attributable to the uncontrolled coupling geometry: the open SMA cable does not
deliver a well-defined $V_\mathrm{AC}$ to the junction, making the effective RF
voltage a non-trivial function of the attenuator setting.

The full $V(I, P)$ and $dV/dI(I, P)$ colormaps are shown in
\hyperref[fig:task6map]{Figure~\ref*{fig:task6map}}. At low power
($P < \SI{0}{\dBm}$), the central superconducting region exhibits uniformly low
$dV/dI$, while the switching transitions at $|I| \approx \SI{75}{\micro\ampere}$
dominate. Above the onset threshold, the central-region $dV/dI$ increases from
$\sim\!\SI{0.37}{\ohm}$ to $\sim\!\SI{0.47}{\ohm}$, reflecting the microwave-induced disruption
of the zero-voltage plateau into discrete Shapiro steps.


\section{Conclusion}

We characterized a YBCO DC SQUID across six experimental tasks. The principal
quantitative results are: $I_c = \SI{82}{\micro\ampere}$,
$M = \SI{24.9}{\pico\henry}$, and $\Phi_0 = \SI{1.71e-15}{\weber}$ (17.3\%
deviation from theory). The KDE-based Shapiro step extraction, which replaces
arbitrary prominence thresholds with a continuous density estimator using
LSCV-optimised bandwidth selection on the central voltage region, produces stable results
across all frequencies and power levels. At \SI{10}{\giga\hertz}, the extracted
$\Delta V = \SI{19.7}{\micro\volt}$ deviates by only 4.8\% from theory, whereas the
old ad-hoc method yielded values between \SI{13}{\micro\volt} and
\SI{26}{\micro\volt} depending on parameter choices.

The dominant source of systematic error is the uncontrolled microwave coupling geometry (open SMA cable), which introduces frequency- and
power-dependent impedance mismatches. Future improvements should include a matched
antenna or waveguide coupler, temperature-stabilized operation, and uncertainty
propagation from the KDE bandwidth selection through the linear fit.

\section{KDE and LSCV Formulae}
\label{app:kde}

\paragraph{Kernel Density Estimator.}
Given $N$ samples $\{x_1, \ldots, x_N\}$, the Gaussian KDE with bandwidth $h$ is
\begin{equation}
    \hat{f}_h(x) = \frac{1}{Nh}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \varphi\!\left(\frac{x - x_i}{h}\right),
    \label{eq:kde}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi(u) = (2\pi)^{-1/2} e^{-u^2/2}$ is the standard Gaussian kernel.

\paragraph{Silverman's Rule of Thumb.}
The MISE-optimal bandwidth for a Gaussian reference distribution is
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{Silv}} = 0.9\,\min\!\bigl(\hat\sigma,\,
      \mathrm{IQR}/1.34\bigr)\, N^{-1/5}.
    \label{eq:silverman}
\end{equation}
This oversmooths multimodal distributions because $R(g'')$ is underestimated
when $g$ is not Gaussian.

\paragraph{Least-Squares Cross-Validation.}
LSCV selects $h$ by minimising a computable proxy for the MISE:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{LSCV}(h) = R(\hat{f}_h) - \frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
      \hat{f}_{-i,h}(x_i),
    \label{eq:lscv_app}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{f}_{-i,h}$ is the leave-one-out estimator (sample $x_i$ excluded).
The constant term $R(g) = \int g^2(x)\,dx$ is independent of $h$ and drops out.

For Gaussian kernels, the first term has a closed form via the convolution
identity $\varphi * \varphi = \varphi_{\sqrt{2}}$:
\begin{equation}
    R(\hat{f}_h) = \frac{1}{N^2 \sqrt{2}\, h}
      \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}
      \varphi\!\left(\frac{x_i - x_j}{\sqrt{2}\,h}\right).
    \label{eq:rfhat}
\end{equation}
The second term is
\begin{equation}
    \hat{f}_{-i,h}(x_i) = \frac{1}{(N-1)\,h}
      \sum_{\substack{j=1 \\ j \neq i}}^{N}
      \varphi\!\left(\frac{x_i - x_j}{h}\right).
    \label{eq:loo}
\end{equation}
The bandwidth is found by grid search over 40 log-spaced candidates
from $0.05\,h_{\mathrm{Silv}}$ to $2\,h_{\mathrm{Silv}}$.
