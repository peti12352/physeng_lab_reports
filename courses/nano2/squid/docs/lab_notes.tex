\section{Single Junction V-I Characteristics (Tasks 1 and 2)}
\paragraph{Experimental Setup} The sample was cooled into the superconducting (SC) regime. Measurements were driven by a \SI{120}{\micro\ampere} triangular wave bias current through a \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm} bias resistor, producing a linear current sweep.
\paragraph{Results} The transition mapped the energy gap $\Delta(T) \propto T_c \sqrt{1 - T/T_c}$, indicating the binding energy of Cooper pairs. Parameter extraction from the threshold yielded a critical current $I_c = \SI{82}{\micro\ampere}$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:figures}, Fig.~\ref{fig:vi_curves}). 

\section{SQUID Flux Response and Inductance (Task 3)}
\paragraph{Setup} A \SI{41}{Hz} triangle drive modulated the flux bias.
\paragraph{Results} The interference response occurred at approximately \SI{400 \pm 100}{Hz}, signalling traversal across multiple flux quanta within a single sweep (see Appendix~\ref{app:figures}, Fig.~\ref{fig:flux}).
\paragraph{Mutual Inductance Extraction} Automated peak detection on 14 analyzed periods yielded bias periods $\Delta V_{\mathrm{ref}} = \SI{0.8293}{V}$ and $\Delta I_{\mathrm{flux}} = \SI{82.93}{\micro\ampere}$. Using $\Delta I_{\mathrm{flux}} = \Phi_0/M$, the extracted mutual inductance is $M = \SI{24.94}{pH}$, consistent with manual estimations.

\section{Shapiro Resonances: Frequency Sweep (Tasks 4 and 5)}
\paragraph{Objective} Verification of the AC Josephson effect relation $\Delta V = \frac{h}{2e}\nu = \Phi_0 \nu$.
\paragraph{Setup} The SQUID was irradiated with a microwave field; frequency $\nu$ was swept from \SI{8}{GHz} to \SI{12}{GHz}. Differential amplifier gain ($A \approx 1000\times$) was compensated to retrieve absolute quantum voltage scales.
\paragraph{Data Processing} Step size ($\Delta V$) extraction utilized two parallel cross-verifying methodologies: transition edge detection via differential resistance maxima ($dV/dI$ peaks), and plateau centre evaluation via voltage density of states (histogram peaks). While physically grounded, the reliance on fixed peak-prominences and histogram bin-widths introduces ad-hoc heuristics. Implementing adaptive noise-floor thresholding (e.g., $3\sigma_{\text{noise}}$) or Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) would rigorously automate and eliminate bias.
\paragraph{Results and Error} Visualisation of the \SI{10}{GHz} step extraction workflow is given in Appendix~\ref{app:figures}, Fig.~\ref{fig:shapiro}. The frequency dependence yielded an extracted $\Phi_0 = \SI{1.88e-15}{Wb}$ (Error: $\sim 9.2\%$). However, despite this seemingly low aggregate error, the underlying $\Delta V$ vs.\ $\nu$ data exhibits severe scatter (see Appendix~\ref{app:figures}, Fig.~\ref{fig:freq_dep}). The ad-hoc nature of the fixed-prominence edge detection fails to adapt to varying noise floors and impedance mismatches across the frequency sweep, resulting in catastrophic misidentification of plateau boundaries. Consequently, the extracted voltage step sizes are highly unstable and erratic, demonstrating the fragility of rigid heuristic processing on raw cryogenic signals.

\section{Shapiro Resonances: Power Sweep (Task 6)}
\paragraph{Setup} Microwave frequency fixed at \SI{10}{GHz} whilst iterating driving power levels. 28 measured step points were mapped onto 8 discrete power tiers (\SI{-26.1}{dBm} to \SI{11.7}{dBm}).
\paragraph{Processing} Microwave driver power $P$ was systematically interpolated across the measurement index $N$ using a robust 3rd-order polynomial map $P(N) = a_3 N^3 + a_2 N^2 + a_1 N + a_0$. Extracted $\Delta V$ magnitudes from histogram/derivative methods were mapped sequentially against the interpolated indices.
\paragraph{Results} The cubic polynomial ($a_3 \approx 3.78\times 10^{-3}$, $a_2 \approx -0.20$, $a_1 \approx 3.90$, $a_0 \approx -25.22$) generated a reliable stability curve (RSS $\approx 7.53$) verifying standardisation without exhibiting Runge's phenomenon or high-frequency over-oscillation (see Appendix~\ref{app:figures}, Fig.~\ref{fig:power_dep}).
